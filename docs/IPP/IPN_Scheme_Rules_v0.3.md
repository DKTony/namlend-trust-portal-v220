3. Purpose of the Scheme Rules

3.1. This document outlines the Scheme Rules for the Instant Payment Solution (IPS)

of Namibia that is being operationalised by the entity Instant Payments Namibia

(IPN). These Scheme Rules are issued to define the legal and regulatory

framework under which the Scheme will operate, to determine the management of

its participants, to provide compliance and risk management guidelines, and to

establish operational parameters for participation, including fees & charges,

among others.

3.2. These Scheme Rules are issued pursuant to Section 12 of the Payment System

Management Act, 2023 (Act No. 14 of 2023) (hereinafter referred to as “PSM Act”),

which mandates a payment system operator to have rules that govern the payment

system. In this regard, these Scheme Rules, together with the IPS Product Rules

and IPS Operator Service Level Agreement (SLA), establish the regulatory,

operational, and technological framework that governs the IPS ecosystem.

3.3. The IPS is a national, interoperable payment infrastructure established by the Bank

of Namibia (BoN) and operationalised through IPN, in close collaboration with

licensed industry participants. The IPS enables instant, 24/7/365 electronic

payments, supporting seamless fund transfers between bank accounts, e-money

wallets, and other approved store-of-value instruments. By enabling real-time low-

value retail payments, the IPS contributes to broader national objectives of

financial inclusion, digital transformation, and the development of safe and efficient

payment systems, particularly for underserved communities in rural and informal

sectors.

6

4. Scope of Application

4.1. These Rules apply to all entities authorised to participate in the IPS ecosystem,

including:

a) Licensed Banking Institutions as defined in the Banking Institutions Act, 2023 (Act

No. 13 of 2023) (or the “BIA”),

b) Licensed Non-Bank Financial Institutions (NBFIs) licensed to provide payment

services under PSD-1,

c) Prospective and authorised System Participants of the IPS in terms of the Act and

PSD-6.

d) Any other third-party vendors that are critical to the operations of the IPN.

6. Governance and Jurisdiction

6.1. The IPS is operated by Instant Payments Namibia (IPN), a subsidiary of the Bank

of Namibia established to operationalise instant payment services. IPN is governed

by the IPN Board which reports to the Bank of Namibia. From an executive

management perspective, the IPN is led by a Chief Operating Officer, supported

by three heads (Operations, IT, and Product & Ecosystem Activation), and a

supporting staff.

6.2. The IPS operates within the jurisdiction of the Republic of Namibia and is subject

to the laws and regulatory requirements applicable within the national payment

system. Participation in the IPS is therefore restricted to entities that are duly

licensed or authorised to operate within Namibia in accordance with the PSM Act,

and any relevant regulatory instruments issued by the Bank of Namibia.

6.3.

IPN, as the IPS Operator, licensed and authorised to operate a payment system

in Namibia, shall be responsible for the day-to-day administration and enforcement

of the IPS Scheme Rules. This includes monitoring participant compliance,

administering access controls, implementing technical upgrades, and ensuring

adherence to system standards. The Operator shall further be mandated to

oversee dispute resolution mechanisms, fraud prevention protocols, and systemic

risk mitigation efforts, thereby ensuring the long-term integrity, security, and

sustainability of the IPS within the Namibian jurisdiction. Nothing precludes the IPS

Operator from contracting with any other licensed payment system operator in

executing its functions as an Operator.

7. Applicable Legal and Regulatory Framework

7.1. The IPS is governed under the following legal instruments:

a) The Payment System Management Act, 2023, provides the enabling legal

framework for the authorisation of payment system operators and the rules

governing payment systems in Namibia.

b) PSD-6 (Determination for the Authorisation of Payment System Operators and

System Participants), which outlines eligibility and compliance requirements for

payment system operators and their system participants.

c) PSD-1, governing licensing requirements for payment service providers and e-

money issuers.

d) Additional compliance obligations under the Financial Intelligence Act (2012, as

amended), the Competition Act (2003, as amended), the Data Protection

Guidelines, and the Consumer Protection Framework are applicable to all payment

service providers.

7.2.

Instant Payment Solution Participants (IPSPs) are required to comply not only with

these Scheme Rules but also with any directives, circulars, or technical standards

issued by the IPS Operator or the Bank of Namibia from time to time.

8. Participant Management

8.1.

Introduction

This section provides a framework for participation management, including onboarding,

participation types, ongoing participation, the rights and duties of all participants, as well

as the processes for suspension and termination.

8.2. Type of Participants

8.2.1.

The Instant Payment Solution (IPS) enables a multi-tiered participation model

designed to promote interoperability, innovation, and inclusive access across a

wide range of financial institutions and service providers. Participation in the

IPS is open to a range of licensed entities within the Namibian payment

ecosystem. For the purposes of the IPS Scheme Rules, participants are

categorised into two broad institutional classes as follows:

a) Store of Value Providers (SoV)

b) Instant Payment Service Providers (IPSP)

8.2.2.

Store of Value (SoV) Providers

There are two types of SoV providers, namely banking institutions and non-banking

financial institutions licensed to issue payment instruments. These are participants

authorised to hold customer funds and provide accounts or wallets into which IPS

transactions are credited or debited. SoV Providers are responsible for:

a) Opening and maintaining customer accounts or e-wallets,

b) Receiving and processing debit or credit instructions initiated via the IPS switch,

c) Confirming the outcome of such instructions in real-time.

11

Only Banks and licensed PIIs may serve as Store of Value Providers within the IPS

framework. The IPS distinguishes between participants based on functional roles within

the system, which determine the extent of their engagement with the IPS infrastructure.

8.2.3.

Instant Payment System Participants (IPSPs)

These are entities directly connected to the IPS switch, capable of initiating and/or

receiving payment instructions on behalf of their own customers, enabling store-of-value

providers, or providing IPS services. IPSPs must:

a) Fulfil all technical onboarding requirements and certification processes,

b) Ensure adherence to IPS messaging formats, service level agreements (SLAs),

and uptime requirements,

c) Be either a Bank or a licensed PII with the operational and technical capability to

participate in real-time payments.

A single entity may serve as both an IPSP and a SoV Provider, provided it meets the

technical requirements of both roles.

8.3. Settlement System Participants

8.3.1.

IPS transactions will settle in the NISS, where SoV Providers are required to

have dedicated settlement accounts to settle interbank obligations that arise

from the IPS in real-time.

8.3.2.

Both direct participation and tiered participation will be accommodated for IPS

transactions. A direct system participant is required to have a settlement

account with the Bank of Namibia to settle its obligations and those of system

participants it intends to sponsor. A sponsored system participant is required

to nominate a direct participant to settle obligations on its behalf in the NISS.

12

8.3.3.

IPSPs that intend to be direct or indirect participants in the RTGS should meet

all technical, liquidity, and operational requirements prescribed by the RTGS

(NISS) Operator.

8.4. Membership Application, Decision, and Appeal Process

8.4.1.

Eligibility for Authorisation

a) Only entities authorised by the IPS Operator may participate in the IPS.

b) Only licensed banks and non-banks in terms of section 16.2 of PSD-6 may

be authorised participants of the IPS by the IPS Operator.

8.4.2.

Application Requirements

a) In terms of sections 17 and 18 of PSD-6, any entity seeking authorisation

to become a system participant in the IPS must first submit a formal letter

of intent to the Bank of Namibia, addressed to the Director responsible for

the National Payment System. This letter must clearly state the applicant’s

intention to engage the IPS Operator for system participation.

b) The letter of intent of a non-bank payment service provider must be

supported by a business plan detailing:

i.

The nature and scope of the intended payment services to be offered

through the IPS.

ii. System schematics and architecture, including transaction flows,

functionalities, data processing, and security infrastructure.

iii. Any third-party or outsourcing arrangements relevant to service

provision.

iv. A comprehensive risk assessment for the proposed services.

v. Consumer protection measures, including onboarding protocols,

pricing structure, and redress mechanisms.

13

vi.

Information security controls and vulnerability management

processes.

vii. A financial forecast covering the first three years of operations.

viii. Any additional information as may be requested by the Regulator.

c) The Bank of Namibia will assess the submission and, if deemed

satisfactory, may issue a letter of no objection, granting the applicant

permission to engage the IPS Operator for authorisation.

d) Upon receipt of the letter of no objection, the applicant may formally initiate

engagement with the IPS Operator for authorisation in accordance with

Section 19.1 of PSD-6.

e) The IPS Operator, upon receipt of the letter of no objection, will enter into a

Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) with the applicant. Thereafter, the IPS

Operator will provide the applicant with access to the Functional

Specification Document (FSD), Technical Specification Document (TSD)

and any other relevant documentation required by the applicant to facilitate

system integration and compliance readiness.

8.4.3.

Application Process

a) Any entity seeking to participate in the IPS must submit a formal membership

application to the IPS Operator. The application process will be automated.

b) The full application must include, at a minimum, the following:

i.

Letter of no objection to engage the IPS Operator received from Bank of

Namibia (The Regulator);

ii. A formal letter of application signed by an authorised official (e.g., CEO, CFO,

or Compliance Officer);

iii. BIPA founding statements of the entity (non-banks only);

14

iv. Banking license in terms of the BIA and/or Payment Service Provider License

(Payment Instrument Issuer category) in terms of PSD-1;

v. Any other relevant license or authorisation;

vi. Nature of intended participation sought, i.e., SoV Provider or IPSP;

vii. Proposed settlement arrangement (direct or via sponsoring participant);

viii.

In the case of a tiered participation arrangement, submit the SLA with the

sponsoring participant;

ix. Corporate governance structure, including a list of directors and executive

management (non-banks only);

x. Technical readiness and system integration capability, including interface

specifications;

xi. Penetration test (from a reputable independent assessor) results of the

infrastructure integrating with the IPS central switch;

xii. Business continuity, data protection, and cybersecurity measures; and

xiii. Any other information that the IPS Operator may request to finalise the

assessment.

c) The onboarding process involves reviewing the application, verifying compliance,

conducting technical integration testing, and obtaining final approval from the IPS

Operator. Participants must complete a due diligence assessment that evaluates

their technical capability to provide IPS services and risk management procedures.

d) Participation in the IPS testing and certification process includes:

i.

ii.

Integration with the sandbox (Simulator) environment;

Live testing for transaction flows and reconciliation;

iii. Confirmation of settlement routing and messaging standards; and

iv. Certification.

e) Formal admission into the IPS ecosystem upon successful completion of tests,

signing of the participant agreement, and issuance of production credentials.

15

f) All application submissions must be complete and compliant with the minimum

Communication of Final Decision and Next Steps

a) Once the Bank of Namibia has endorsed the IPS Operator’s decision, the IPS

b) If the application is approved, the IPS Operator shall provide the applicant with the

i. Execution of legal agreements

ii. Payment of applicable joining fees as prescribed by the IPN

iii. System integration and connectivity timelines

iv.

Testing and certification requirements; and

v. Operational readiness procedures.

including certification

testing,

Appeal Process

9.  Rights and Duties of Scheme Participants

9.1.

Introduction

9.1.1.

This section outlines the binding rights and duties of participants admitted into

the Instant Payment Solution (IPS) Scheme. These rights and duties are

informed by the overarching regulatory framework under the PSM Act, the

associated Bank of Namibia Determinations (PSD-1, PSD-6, etc.), and the

operational governance of the IPS Operator. Participants are required to

always observe these rules.

9.1.2.

Non-compliance may result in corrective measures, including warnings, fines,

restrictions, suspensions and/or termination of scheme membership.

9.2. Rights of Scheme Participants

All authorised IPS participants shall have the following rights:

9.2.1.

Right to Access Scheme Infrastructure

17

Participants shall have complete and non-discriminatory access to the IPS

infrastructure, provided they meet the applicable eligibility, technical, and

operational requirements.

9.2.2.

Right to Participate in Scheme Governance

Participants may be invited to participate in forums and working groups established

by the IPS Operator for the purpose of rule amendments, system enhancements,

or performance reviews. Voting rights, where applicable, will be limited to

categories defined in the IPN governance structure.

9.2.3.

Right to Transparent Information

Participants have the right to receive timely and transparent information on:

a) Changes to scheme rules or operating procedures;

b) Scheduled maintenance, downtime, or major incidents; and

c) Performance metrics or compliance benchmarks.

9.2.4.

Right to Withdraw from the Scheme

Participants may voluntarily terminate their membership by providing the IPS Operator

with a minimum of 90 calendar days' written notice. Exit will be subject to regulatory

approval and the settlement of all financial obligations and compliance with the exit

procedure, including:

d) Exit audit; and

e) Submission of transaction and settlement reconciliation reports.

9.3. Duties of Scheme Participants

Participation in the IPS is a privilege that carries with it several responsibilities,

including the duty to maintain compliance, uphold system integrity, and support

overall scheme performance.

18

9.3.1.

Compliance with Legal and Regulatory Obligations

Participants shall:

a) Ensure continuous compliance with the PSM Act, 2023, the Banking Institutions

Act, 2023, PSD-1, PSD-6, and all relevant laws;

b) Maintain good standing with the Bank of Namibia,; and

c) Submit any supervisory reviews, inspections, or information requests initiated

by the Bank or IPS Operator.

9.3.2.

Cooperation with the IPS Operator

Participants shall:

a) Respond timeously to all reasonable requests for information from the IPS

Operator;

b) Cooperate

in system-wide activities,

including

incident

investigations,

operational reviews, and industry benchmarking initiatives; and

c) Share relevant data needed for risk assessment, scheme enhancements, or

market development, subject to confidentiality provisions.

9.3.3.

All system participants must:

a) Comply fully with the IPS Scheme Rules and any supplementary guidance,

manuals, or circulars issued by the IPS Operator.

b) Maintain ongoing compliance with applicable laws and regulations, including

AML/CFT, data protection, and consumer protection legislation.

c) Submit required reports, including compliance updates, technical performance

audits, and incident notifications.

d) Cooperate with the IPS Operator and the Bank of Namibia during investigations,

audits, and system upgrades.

e) Maintain uninterrupted system connectivity and operational availability

in

accordance with service-level requirements.

9.3.4.

Operational Performance and Settlement

19

Participants must:

a) Maintain 99.9% system availability and real-time processing capabilities in line with

IPS technical and service-level requirements;

b) Ensure accurate and timely settlement of all obligations arising from IPS

transactions;

c) Submit reconciliation and settlement data daily or as otherwise prescribed by the

IPS Operator.

d) Provide other IPSPs with adequate and timely settlement information, including

but not limited to transaction identifiers, timestamps, error notifications, and

confirmation messages to enable accurate counterparty reconciliation;

e) Ensure that settlement-related systems, files, and interfaces remain consistently

available during core IPS operational hours to facilitate seamless inter-participant

clearing and settlement processes.

9.3.5.

Risk and Incident Reporting

Participants shall:

a) Promptly report to the IPS Operator any incident that could pose a material risk to

the scheme, including but not limited to: data breaches, service disruptions, fraud

incidents, and compliance breaches;

b) Provide a formal incident report within 48 hours of any qualifying event, including

root cause, immediate corrective measures, and mitigation plans;

c) Maintain a record of incidents and provide regular updates on incident resolution

status upon request.

9.3.6.

Participation in Reviews and Audits

Participants shall:

a) Undergo periodic membership reviews as required by the IPS Operator to evaluate

continued compliance and system fitness;

b) Participate and render support during audits initiated by the IPS Operator;

20

c) Submit operational reports, including:

i) Performance and stress test results;

ii) Penetration test summaries (annually).

9.3.7.

Respect for Scheme Interoperability and Competition Principles

Participants must:

a) Avoid anti-competitive behaviour and practices that could undermine the

interoperability or neutrality of the IPS;

b) Ensure fair access to customers across the IPS network, including indirect

participants and enablers.

9.3.8.

Obligation to Pay Prescribed Fees

Participants are required to pay all applicable fees as prescribed in the Fee Book of the

IPS Scheme Rules and as stipulated by the Bank of Namibia.

9.3.9.

Amendments to Rights and Duties

a)

The IPS Operator, in consultation with the Bank of Namibia, reserves the right to

amend or expand these rights and duties. Any material changes will be

communicated to participants at least 30 business days in advance, unless earlier

enforcement is required by law or regulation.

b) Participants will have the opportunity to comment on proposed amendments

through structured consultation forums. Failure to adhere to amended rules, once

adopted, may result in penalties as outlined in the scheme enforcement

framework.

10. Suspension and Termination of Scheme Participation

21

10.1. Introduction

10.1.1. The integrity, efficiency, and security of the IPS Scheme depend on the

continuous compliance of all participants with the IPS Scheme Rules, product

rules, operational standards, and applicable

regulatory

requirements.

Accordingly, the IPS Operator reserves the right to suspend or terminate a

participant’s access to the Scheme under the conditions outlined below.

10.1.2. Suspension or termination may arise from voluntary, regulatory, or non-

compliance-based causes, and shall be exercised in line with the principles of

due process, proportionality, and transparency, subject to oversight by the

Bank of Namibia.

10.2. Grounds for Suspension or Termination

A participant may be suspended or terminated from the IPS Scheme under the following

circumstances:

10.2.1. Non-compliance with Scheme Rules or Regulatory Requirements

a) Persistent or material breach of Scheme Rules, product rules, TSD, or FSD

obligations;

b) Failure to provide accurate or timely transaction, settlement, or incident reports;

c) Inability or refusal to cooperate with system investigations, audits, or compliance

reviews;

d) Failure to maintain regulatory licensing under PSD-1 or the Banking Institutions

Act; or

e) Contravention of the PSM Act or any determination issued by the Bank of Namibia.

10.2.2. Financial Default or Non-Payment

a) Repeated failure to meet settlement obligations or to pay scheme-related fees;

22

b)

Insolvency, liquidation, or entry into administration; or

c) Suspension or revocation of the participant’s settlement account by the settlement

agent.

10.2.3. Mandate from a Competent Authority

a) Directive or ruling from a judicial, regulatory, or supervisory authority (e.g., High

Court, Bank of Namibia, NamRA, NamFISA, or Financial Intelligence Centre); or

b) Revocation of payment service provider license.

10.2.4. Threats to System Integrity

12.2. Reconciliation and Settlement

12.2.1. Purpose

a) This section outlines the rules governing reconciliation and settlement within the

Instant Payment Solution (IPS). These processes are foundational to ensuring

secure, efficient, and transparent fund transfers between IPS participants. The IPS

Operator governs a centralised back-office platform known as the IPS Real-Time

Clearing and Settlement (IRCS) system, which ensures automated reconciliation,

generation of settlement files, and execution of net multilateral settlements through

the Namibia Interbank Settlement System (NISS).

b) The settlement process is designed to support both direct and indirect participation

models. Direct participants maintain settlement accounts in the NISS, which is

38

operated by the Bank of Namibia. Indirect participants are sponsored through a

direct participant's NISS account.

12.2.2. Onboarding to the Reconciliation and Settlement Platform

a) In addition to onboarding on the IPS Switch platform, all participants are required

to onboard onto the IRCS system to ensure full participation in the end-to-end

transaction lifecycle, including settlement and reporting.

b) The onboarding process includes:

i.

Issuance of Participant Identification Codes (PICs)

ii. Setup and configuration of settlement accounts (direct or sponsored)

iii. User creation and permission assignment on the IRCS

iv. Connectivity and file exchange configuration (e.g., FTP/SFTP)

c) The IPS Operator is responsible for managing and facilitating the onboarding

process in accordance with system and security protocols. Only upon completion

of onboarding to both the switching and settlement platforms may a participant be

considered operationally live within the IPS ecosystem.

12.2.3. Participant Identification

a) To ensure accurate routing and settlement, the IPS Operator issues each IPS

participant a Participant Identification Code (PIC). The PIC is alphanumeric and

formatted as follows:

i. First 4 characters: BIC name (letters only)

39

ii. Fifth character: Identifier (0 for banks, 1 for non-banks)

iii.

Last 6 characters: Unique numeric code

b) For settlement purposes, these PICs are matched to the participant’s SWIFT code,

which will be used in the Multilateral Net Settlement Batch (MNSB) file generation.

While participants are not required to have BIC codes unless they are direct

settlement participants, all participants are identifiable in the IRCS regardless of

their tier (i.e., direct or indirect). The IPS Operator is responsible for assigning and

maintaining these codes.

12.2.4. Reconciliation Process

a) All completed, deemed, or unsuccessful transactions processed through the IPS

Switch are routed to the IRCS for reconciliation. The reconciliation process

involves:

i. Automatic syncing of transaction records between the IPS Switch and the

IRCS.

ii. Comparison of raw data files, CBS files, and IPS Switch files by participants.

iii.

Identification and resolution of discrepancies by participants via the operations

portal.

b) In cases of deemed transactions, participants must:

i. Raise a Transaction Credit Confirmation (TCC) if the beneficiary has been

credited.

ii. Raise a Return (RET) to initiate reversal if no credit occurred.

iii. Ensure the originating participant issues a Debit Reversal Confirmation (DRC)

after a return is effected.

c) Reconciliation is fully automated, and summary outputs are made available to

administrative users for transparency and monitoring.

40

12.2.5. Settlement Framework

a) The IPS operates a centralised multilateral net settlement system, where

obligations between participants are netted and settled via the NISS. The formal

Settlement Agency for IPS transactions is the Bank of Namibia, which administers

the Namibia Interbank Settlement System (NISS), managing file generation and

submission.

b) All IPS participants are required to maintain a settlement account with the NISS at

the Bank of Namibia. Participants without direct access to NISS will participate via

a sponsoring institution (tiered participation). The sponsor bears responsibility for

the obligations and positions of any indirect participants it represents.

c) Settlement is conducted through a Multilateral Net Settlement Batch (MNSB) file,

generated in ISO 20022 format (i.e.pacs.009). The MNSB file includes:

i. Net payable/receivable values

ii.

Interchange fees

iii. Switching fees owed to the IPS Operator

iv. Any penalties or adjustments

d) As the appointed Settlement Agency, the Bank of Namibia serves as:

i.

ii.

The intermediary between participants and the IPS Operator

The processor and final authority for posting net obligations to participants’

settlement accounts

iii.

The institution that guarantees settlement finality

e) All IPS settlements occur via the submission of ISO-standard MNSB files to the

NISS (in batches) through a secure channel. Settlement entries submitted to NISS

41

are final and irrevocable. This partnership ensures regulatory oversight, finality,

and alignment with national payment infrastructure policies.

12.2.6. Settlement Models and Cycles

a) The IPS settlement process follows a Deferred Net Settlement (DNS) model.

Under DNS, transactions are aggregated over a defined period and netted before

submission to the NISS. This method is efficient, minimises liquidity requirements,

and supports batch-based reconciliation.

b) The Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) model, where each transaction is settled

individually in real time, is not employed for IPS transactions. Instead, RTGS is

used downstream at the NISS level, where the final multilateral settlement

amounts are posted to each participant’s NISS account in real-time based on the

netted values calculated by IRCS.

c) Thus, while IPS itself does not process individual RTGS settlements per

transaction, the clearing file submitted to NISS is processed using RTGS

infrastructure, ensuring finality and immediacy once files are accepted.

d) Settlement cycles (windows) are pre-configured and automated via IRCS. For the

initial go-live phase, the following cycles will be active:

Weekdays (Monday to Friday):

• SW1: 08:00

• SW2: 12:00

• SW3: 15:00

Saturdays:

• SW1: 11:00

42

e) Additional windows (e.g., 19:00 weekdays, 15:00 Saturdays) are configured for

future enablement based on system demands and participant readiness.

a) File delivery is automated via FTP and structured to support both human-readable

formats (CSV) and machine-readable ISO 20022 messages.

12.2.7. Settlement Currency

a) All IPS settlements are denominated in Namibia Dollars (NAD) and conducted

through NISS. Should cross-border functionalities be introduced in future scheme

enhancements, the IPS Operator will define the applicable currency conversion

methodologies.

12.2.8. Reporting and Reconciliation Outputs

a) Participants receive daily settlement reports generated by the IRCS, which include:

i. Raw Data File: All individual transactions (remitter and beneficiary views only)

ii. Net Settlement Report (NTSL): Net positions, interchange, switching fees

iii. Adjustment File: Raised or received disputes

iv. Pending Status Reports: Transactions awaiting resolution

v. Timeout Reports: Transactions exceeding time limits

b) These reports are disseminated simultaneously with the submission of settlement

instructions to NISS. Participants are required to verify these files; however,

12.2.9. Dispute and Adjustment Handling

a) Participants may raise disputes through the IRCS operations portal:

i. Single dispute entry or bulk file upload

ii. Visibility into both raised and received disputes

43

iii. Receipt of Adjustment Reports reflecting all dispute activities

b) Disputed transactions are excluded from the current settlement cycle and resolved

ii. Roles and access privilege configuration

iii. Dispute type and fee structure definitions

b) Access to IRCS modules is managed by Admin users with role-based permissions,

12.2.11. Liquidity Management

a) The IPS provides each participant with visibility of their exposure values per

i. Participants see only their own exposures

ii. The IPS Operator and Bank of Namibia may view system-wide exposures

b) This visibility supports proactive liquidity planning and management.

12.4.3. Participant-Level Monitoring

capabilities in accordance with these Scheme Rules and applicable laws.

These internal monitoring systems shall be capable of:

i. Detecting transaction threshold breaches or anomalies

46

ii.

Identifying repeated reversals, excessive refund patterns, or transaction

disputes

iii.

Tracking channel-specific usage (e.g. mobile, online, USSD) for emerging

risks

12.6. Participants must maintain logs of their monitoring outcomes and submit

reports to IPN as per the frequency and format prescribed in the IPS Operating

Guidelines. IPN reserves the right to request additional information or initiate

joint investigations into systemic risks identified during participant-level

monitoring.

12.6.1. Fraud Checks and Related Monitoring

a) IPS participants shall be individually responsible for conducting all pre-

transaction and post-transaction fraud checks within their own environments.

These checks include, but are not limited to:

i. Know Your Customer (KYC) verification

ii. Customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence (EDD)

iii. Anti-Money Laundering (AML) screening (customer level)

iv. Counter-Terrorism Financing (CTF) controls

v. Sanctions list screening in line with Namibian laws and international

standards (e.g., FATF guidelines)

b) For real-time transaction processing, participants must ensure that these

checks are completed either prior to transaction initiation or in parallel during

processing to avoid compromising processing times.

c) IPN will issue scheme-level fraud prevention rules and parameters that

participants must incorporate into their risk models, including:

47

i. Guidance on average transaction values per channel or customer type

ii.

Threshold limits requiring additional review or EDD

iii. Reversal limits per customer or per channel

iv. Red

flags related

to

IP address, device, or geographical usage

inconsistencies

d) Compliance with these parameters will be subject to review during periodic risk

assessments, scheme audits, or supervisory reviews coordinated by IPN.

12.6.2. Central Oversight and Reporting

a) IPN shall collect and analyse monitoring data across all participants to generate

trend analyses, early warning indicators, and system-wide risk assessments.

b) IPN shall share relevant fraud intelligence with IPS participants, in line with

applicable data protection and confidentiality obligations,

to promote

collaborative fraud mitigation.

c) The effectiveness of transaction monitoring and fraud mitigation activities shall

be reviewed annually by IPN and may be subject to audit and oversight by the

IPN Board or designated regulatory bodies.

d) To ensure compliance with operational and regulatory standards, the IPS

Operator will implement a continuous transaction monitoring system. This

system will analyse transaction flows, detect unusual patterns, and provide

participants with compliance reports. In cases of significant compliance

breaches or security threats, participants will be required to take immediate

corrective action and report incidents to the IPS Operator within prescribed

timelines.

14. Technology

14.1. Objective and Overview of the Technology Framework

14.1.1. The Instant Payment Solution (IPS) of Namibia is designed as a 24/7/365 real-

time payment ecosystem, enabling secure, always-on, and interoperable digital

payments. The technology framework outlined herein defines the architecture,

standards, and obligations of all system participants to ensure continuous

service availability, resilience, and security.

14.1.2. The IPS is API-based and adheres to international best practices in real-time

payments. All IPS Participants shall comply with the standards and controls

defined in this section and the associated Technical Specification Document

(TSD). Technical non-compliance may result in penalties, suspension, or

termination as per these Scheme Rules.

14.2. Messaging Standards and Communication Protocols

14.2.1. Messaging Standards

a) The IPS uses RESTful APIs for all financial and non-financial transaction flows,

defined

in

the

IPS Functional and Technical Specification Documents.

Standardised payload structures ensure consistent interpretation of messages

across the ecosystem. All APIs use XML schema over HTTPS with clear status

codes and defined error handling procedures.

b) Standard: ISO 20022 message structures are used for settlement to the NISS. A

proprietary standard is used during initial rollout as outlined in the FSD.

14.2.2. Mode of Communication

a) All communication with the IPS switch is via API-based, asynchronous RESTful

services. Participants must support synchronous handshake for payment

50

acknowledgements and asynchronous updates for dispute resolution, settlement

responses, and fraud notifications.

14.2.3. Transaction Types

a) Participants must support credit-push transactions for P2P, P2B, B2P, G2P,

merchant cash-in, and ATM cash-out use cases. Debit-pull models are out of

scope for go-live.

14.2.4. Message Security and Validation

a) All message exchanges shall be digitally signed. Validation of sender authenticity,

payload integrity, and message non-repudiation must be ensured through Public

Key Infrastructure (PKI). Authentication must follow two-factor or tokenised

schemes.

14.3. Technical Infrastructure and Integration Requirements

14.3.1.

Integration Requirements

a) Connectivity to the IPS switch will be provisioned exclusively over the IPN’s MPLS.

The IPS switch will not be exposed to the public Internet under any circumstances.

b) All participants must integrate with:

i. The IPS Central Switch via secure APIs

ii. The IPN Bank Portal for dispute and query management

iii. The back-office portal for reporting and settlement

c) Participants must undergo mandatory integration and certification as outlined in

the FSD.

14.3.2. System Availability and SLAs

a) All IPS Participants must ensure:

51

i.

99.9% system uptime monthly

ii. Transaction processing times under 5 seconds (end-to-end)

iii. API response time under 2 seconds (for 95% of transactions)

b) SLAs shall be monitored centrally. Participants failing to meet SLAs for two

consecutive months shall be subject to performance reviews and possible

sanctions.

14.3.3. Technical Support and Communication

a) The IPS Operator will provide a 24/7 helpdesk and monthly performance

monitoring reports. Participants must appoint a technical single point of contact

(SPOC) and maintain an escalation matrix.

14.4. Contingency and Business Continuity Framework

14.4.1. Contingency Scenarios

a) Participants must have fallback systems for the following events:

i. Connectivity or link failure

ii. Host system failure

iii. Data centre outages

iv. Cybersecurity attacks (DDoS, malware)

14.4.2. Business Continuity Plan

a) Each participant must:

i. Maintain secondary infrastructure capable of taking over operations within 15

minutes

ii. Participate in annual disaster recovery drills

iii. Submit a signed BCP compliance attestation to the IPS Operator annually

52

14.4.3. Recovery Plans

a) Participants shall:

i. Maintain RTO (Recovery Time Objective) of <30 minutes

ii. Maintain RPO (Recovery Point Objective) of zero data loss

iii. Submit incident root-cause analysis within 24 hours of critical failures

14.5. Information Security Standards

14.5.1. Data Encryption and Protection

a) Participants must:

i. Encrypt data in transit and at rest using AES-256 or equivalent

ii. Ensure customer PII, IPS PINs, and transaction payloads are encrypted

14.5.2.

Identity and Account Validation

ii. Device binding and alias linkage must follow MNO and SoV validation protocols

iii. Debit card (last 6 digits) or ID number (last 6 digits) must be verified against

14.5.3. Message Integrity and Non-repudiation

a) Participants must:

iii.

Implement request traceability and duplicate request handling

14.5.4. Protection of Authentication Credentials

a) PINs must:
